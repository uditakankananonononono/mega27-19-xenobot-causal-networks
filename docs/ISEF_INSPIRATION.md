# Winner inspiration: evidence design, not borrowed claims

The user named Natasha Kulviwat as an ISEF inspiration. Society for Science's own profile confirms she received the 2023 Gordon E. Moore Award at ISEF and was a 2024 STS finalist. Her project tested a focused claudin-5/blood-brain-barrier biomarker question in de-identified brain tissue and examined related genes, rather than stopping at a broad AI proposal. Source: https://www.societyforscience.org/regeneron-sts/2024-student-finalists/natasha-kulviwat/ . An independent Columbia Psychiatry profile describes a comparison of donated tissue from people who died by suicide and controls over six months: https://www.columbiapsychiatry.org/news/intern-wins-50-000-prize-research-novel-biomarkers-predict-suicide-risk . The award/project alone does not establish that any biomarker is clinically validated or that our Xenobot questions have data. Do not copy suicide-biomarker biological claims or imply a comparable wet-lab contribution.

Transferable design pattern for our no-wet-lab Xenobot lane: choose one measurable biological signature (rather than umbrella 'intelligence'); compare explicit condition-matched controls; independently confirm the signature and quantify effect/uncertainty; separate mechanistic hypotheses from proof; tie the conclusion strictly to the measured unit. We have no analogous paired cell-level dataset yet. Ask future external judges what specific signature, orthogonal control, independent replication and testable follow-up this lane lacks. A winner archetype is an inspiration for rigor, never a substitute for measured results.
