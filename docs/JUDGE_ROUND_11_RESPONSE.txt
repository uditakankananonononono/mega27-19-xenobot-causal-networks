Round 11 — Novelty review of the revised computational direction
Summary judgment

The project has correctly separated into two tracks:

Flagship biological question:
How do motile Xenobots coordinate, adapt, and potentially store behavioral states through cellular networks?

Status: data-gated.

Immediate computational methods artifact:
How reliably can passive multicellular time-series forecasting distinguish direct interaction from shared latent dynamics?

Status: potentially viable, but not yet a biological discovery.

The novelty opportunity is no longer “decode Xenobot intelligence.” That claim is unsupported by current data. The strongest remaining contribution is a calibration framework showing when cross-cell predictive metrics fail or succeed under known confounds.

Time-series causal benchmarks explicitly recognize the importance of hidden confounders and known ground truth for evaluating causal discovery methods; for example, TimeGraph-type synthetic benchmarks are designed around causal graphs with and without unobserved confounders rather than treating prediction alone as causal evidence.

Primary metrics

Do not make RMSE the main metric.

A model can have lower RMSE while being causally misleading.

Use:

1. False interaction rate

In:

no-coupling + shared-driver condition.

Question:

Does it invent edges?

2. Coupling recovery

In:

direct-coupling condition.

Question:

Does it recover known interactions?

3. Calibration curve

As latent-driver strength increases:

Does false interaction detection increase predictably?

Failure gates

The method fails if:

Shared-driver-only systems repeatedly appear as direct coupling.

Performance depends on knowing the simulation parameters.

Factor models perform equivalently.

Results disappear under realistic noise.

A method only works when the confound is artificially simple.

A negative result here would still be valuable.

TimeGraph issue: what to fix

The mediator issue is important.

Your observation is correct:

Structural direct positive-lag truth and reduced-form VAR lag paths are not automatically equivalent.

A benchmark must define what is being evaluated:

structural causal edge recovery?

predictive lag association?

reduced-form forecasting?

These are different targets.

Time-series causal benchmarks generally emphasize that known ground-truth graphs are needed because observational lag associations can differ from the underlying causal structure, especially with hidden confounding. 
GitHub

Do not compare your method to a table unless the target variable, graph definition, and evaluation metric match.

What would count as a genuine methods novelty?

A strong contribution would be:

"A benchmark and diagnostic framework showing that multicellular forecasting metrics can overestimate interaction under latent shared dynamics, and quantifying the regimes where different models remain reliable."

That is distinct from:

Grasso's functional-network analysis,

Varley's information integration analysis.

Those ask biological questions about measured systems.

This asks:

"Can our inference tool be trusted?"

Different contribution.

What would NOT count as novelty?

Not enough:

"Our model predicts calcium better."

"Our graph has more edges."

"Cells appear connected."

"Information flow increases."

Those are close to already explored analyses and remain vulnerable to passive-data confounding.

Next decision gate

Before investing in a full paper:

A successful methods artifact should have:

A pre-registered benchmark definition.

Known ground truth.

Multiple confound regimes.

Strong simple baselines.

A clearly stated failure mode.

The project’s strongest scientific contribution may be demonstrating why passive multicellular prediction is insufficient for causal claims, rather than claiming that it has solved the Xenobot mechanism problem.
